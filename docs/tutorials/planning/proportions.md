# Proportions

Gundam proportions are difficult; unless you're shaped like a Clamp character, you're going to have to cheat. 
Here are the main problems:
Human legs are shorter than Gundam Legs. 
Gundam Heads are smaller than human heads. 
Our shoulder joints are located closer to the body than gundams. 

*insert clivelee gundam thing here*

As a solution, ther are three general approaches to tackle the proportion problem. None of these are "correct"- they all prioritize and make tradeoffs. Generally, as height/proportional accuracy increases, mobility decreasess. Also keep in mind this has generic gundam proportions in mind- zeon style sutis ar generally stocker, and can sometimes get away with less in the way of additional height. 

1) No platforms. This gives you maximum mobility and comfort, and wears very similar to traditional, non-mech armor builds. However, the shortened legs of the human body really hurt here. The shortened legs means that the torso looks comparatively big even if they are built flush to the body, and the head even more so

2) Medium height platforms (<12"). We start to make sacrifices here- the moment platforms go on, stability and mobility become bigger concerns. Weight also matters quite a bit- three or four pounds might not seem like a lot, but stuck to your feet and dragged across con floors for five, six, seven hours and it begins to add up. Proportion wise, the human feet tends to end up in the ankles of the gundam, letting you add ~25% more length to the lower legs, and have feet that are more or less however tall your platforms are. Longer lower legs means that they are proportioned more accurately to the torso, which can be made a little bit larger. This in turn helps mitiage the large SD-esque head effect. While I have this listed as < 12", generally this refers to all platforms, regardless of height that don't use....

3) Drywall stilts. This is the endgame in cosplay height, and is the tech used by all the crazy builders out there. As a caveat, i've not used these myself, and have only learned about it through second hand knowledge. The first downside is cost- these drywall stilts cost upwards of $100. Secondly, is the skill gap. With platforms, you can more or less walk into them from the jump. Drywall stilts reqwuire an active learning process to get around. Thirdly, drywall stilts have fairly reduced mobility. You might see people jumping around in them online, but at a con these builds move by far the slowest. You most likely won't be able to get onto escalators, stairs are out of the question, and even the elevator will forsake you because you probably won't fit. At heights this tall, the legs become so long that human arms can't keep up, leading to many who use stilts to also use fake hands, meaning no meaningful grabbing.

On the upside, the proportions are greawt. Drywall stils allow you to size the legs perfectly proportionally to your torso. With fake arms, your arms can be perfectly proportioned to the torso. The torso itself can be upsized quite a bit, leading to the most accurate head-size across the three catetgories. The sheer size of these cosplays farm aura. They're identifiable from across the hall, and the proportions make the cosplay as if it leapt off the screen.
