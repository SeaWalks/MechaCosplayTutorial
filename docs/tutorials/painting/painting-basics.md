# Painting Basics

Painting is one of if not the most important step- a plain, undetailed build can be saved with an awesome, high contrast paintjob. A highly detailed build can be crushed by a boring paintjob that doesn't hbighlight the details put in the construction process. 

## Primer
Our primer:
1) Needs to be flexible so it doesn't crack when the foam bends
2) It needs to have other paint stick to it.

Our options: 
- Plasti Dip- A spray on rubber coating that checks those boxes. The community standard for priming EVA foam, and featured on places like Kamui Cosplay, Evil Ted, Punished Props, etc. The main downside is that it isn't really sandable- it will tear up in chunks and leave a really rough texture. 
- Hexflex/Cosflex: *Disclaimer- I have not personally used these products.* Cosplay-oriented sealers, similar to Plasti-Dip. Supposedly sandable- although be careful, since these layers are very thin. One of the big positives is that it comes in a clear- great for sealing Plastazote/light up props. Main downside is price- if you thought Plasti-Dip was bad, imagine it with the Cosplay Tax. 
- Mod Podge/Wood Glue- basically white Elmers glue. While it can work, it can leave brush marks and dries stiff and solid, meaning it will crack if the piece bends. On the upside, it is sandable- so long as there are enough layers to sand. 

> Recommendation: Plasti-Dip; best bang for your buck when it comes to flexible sealers, and Modge Podge/Wood glue's cracking issues aren't worth the savings. Plus, brush application sounds miserable, particularly for large surfaces. 

## Paints
- Spray Paints: The bread and butter that will normally paint the vast majority of your build. For regular colors, most brands of basic spray paints tend to perform the same. I usually pop by walmart and purchase whatever color I need. I will shout-out the Behr range from Home Depot- theyre a bit pricey and don't have a ton of colors compared to RustOLeum/Krylon, but the metallics are fantastic and their matte white in particular is a dead matte white, perfect for Gundam armor. 
- Brush Paints: Using the brush to paint in details can save tons of time in masking and spraying. If you can swing it, paints designed for miniatures work great because they are much more pigmented than their craft store counterparts, meaning fewer coats for higher coverage and better intensity. However, the Applebarrel stuff will work fine enough and be a good deal cheaper. 
> Recommendation: Vallejo paints can usually be purchased from your local friendly games store, or alternatively online for the best price at the Gnomish Bazaar. Craft paints can be found at Walmart. A big pack of cheap synthetic brushes works perfect and can be found on Amazon or the craft store. 
- Airbrush Paints: If you've picked up the Hobby Paints from above, you can thin those down with water or airbrush thinner and spray them quite easily. I prefer based paints from the modeling hobby, mainly Mr Color and Tamiya. They tend to avoid tip and are generally more hassle-free in my experience. However, they need special thinners and can get quite expensive if you need a lot.
